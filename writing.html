ACADEMIC WRITING · EXPOS 20: EVERYDAY FEMINISMS · 2023

BOOK RECS for the GIRLIES going thru it.

Dissociative Feminism on #BookTok

ORIGINAL ESSAY

Read essay (PDF) ↗

Written for Expos 20: Everyday Feminisms, December 2023.

Note: the PDF retains instructor comments and feedback from the submitted document.

The question

How does BookTok turn reading preferences into a feminist identity, and what happens when a counter-tradition built around apathy becomes a visible online community?

The argument

The essay examines “girlboss” popular feminism and the rise of dissociative feminism through BookTok. Drawing on Pierre Bourdieu’s concept of cultural capital, it argues that the literature and audiovisual conventions of these communities can make identities legible while also reproducing exclusions. It considers apathy as a possible critique of performative empowerment, but questions whether that response advances collective feminist change.

Sources & ideas

The essay engages Sarah Banet-Weiser, Pierre Bourdieu, Emmeline Clein, Sofie Behluli, and literary examples including My Year of Rest and Relaxation.

Revision as learning

The accompanying revision letter reflects on the relationship between thinking, writing, and revision. It emphasizes the importance of beginning to think early, revisiting assumptions, and grounding complex arguments in concrete examples.
